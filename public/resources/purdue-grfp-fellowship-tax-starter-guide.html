Starting your fellowship tax plan

A practical starting point for Purdue students moving from a paid RA or TA role to an NSF GRFP or another fellowship that Purdue treats as a “true fellowship.” The first year is usually the confusing one: W-2 wages early in the year, then a stipend with no withholding.

01 · Your starting point

What changed when your assistantship became a fellowship

Purdue describes domestic “True Fellows” as students receiving a stipend to support study or research, rather than compensation for teaching, research, or administrative services. Purdue says these students are not employees and that income tax is not withheld or reported to the IRS on their fellowship payments. That does not settle your whole return, but it explains why the cash flow feels so different.

A useful distinction

No withholding does not mean no tax. IRS Publication 970 explains that a scholarship or fellowship is tax-free only to the extent it meets the applicable requirements, including the qualified-education-expense rules. Amounts used for living expenses such as room and board are generally not qualified education expenses. The taxable part must be reported if you are required to file, even without a W-2.

02 · The messy first year

One tax year can contain two very different pay systems

If you move from an RA/TA position to a fellowship midyear, treat the calendar year as one return with two sources of income, rather than as two separate tax situations. Wages and their withholding remain part of the return; the taxable fellowship amount is added on top.

Keep these records

  • Final W-2 from your assistantship.
  • Fellowship offer / NSF award notice and Purdue appointment information.
  • Every stipend payment record; record the amount and date received.
  • Form 1098-T, if issued, plus tuition-and-fee records.
  • Any federal and Indiana estimated-tax payment confirmations.
  • Prior-year federal and Indiana returns, useful for planning.

Questions to answer before paying

  • How much taxable fellowship income did I actually receive this calendar year?
  • How much federal and Indiana tax was already withheld from my W-2?
  • Will I owe enough after withholding to need estimated payments?
  • Do I have other income, a spouse’s income, dependents, or credits that change the estimate?
  • What county rate applies to my Indiana return?
Do not rely on one person’s numbers

A partial-year fellowship is especially individual. The amount of W-2 withholding, exact deposit dates, health-insurance treatment, filing status, rent, county of residence, and any other income can materially change what you owe. 03 · Build your own plan

A small process is better than guessing once in April

  1. Find the taxable fellowship amount. Start with the award and your actual payment history. Review IRS Publication 970’s fellowship worksheet and Purdue’s information for your award; do not assume every dollar has the same treatment.
  2. Run a full-year estimate. Include wages, taxable fellowship income, other income, deductions, credits, and taxes already withheld. Use the current-year federal Form 1040-ES instructions and Indiana guidance, or have a preparer run the estimate.
  3. Choose the payment method. If estimated payments make sense, federal and Indiana payments are separate. Federal payments can be made through IRS Direct Pay; Indiana uses INTIME or Form ES-40. Check the current due dates and instructions before submitting.
  4. Set aside money as each stipend arrives. A separate savings account and a recurring transfer can make quarterly payments less surprising. Pick a percentage only after running your estimate; a buffer is sensible when inputs are uncertain.
  5. Revisit after your first return. Your completed transition-year return gives you real inputs for the next year. Update the estimate if stipend, residence, rent, other income, or tax law changes.

04 · Filing, software, and IRA questions

Two things worth checking carefully

Reporting the fellowship

For a Form 1040 or 1040-SR filer, the IRS says taxable scholarship or fellowship income that is not reported in W-2 Box 1 belongs on Schedule 1, line 8r. Software menus change, so use a current version and preview the actual forms before filing.

A fellowship is not automatically self-employment income merely because there is no W-2 or 1099. Do not put it on Schedule C unless you have a genuine separate business activity and understand why it belongs there.

IRA eligibility

Taxable fellowship income paid to help an individual pursue graduate or postdoctoral study can count as compensation for IRA-contribution purposes under federal law. That can make a Roth IRA possible, but contribution limits, compensation amount, income limits, and software treatment still need checking.

Read the current IRS Publication 590-A and review Form 5329 in your return if you contribute. This guide is not a recommendation to contribute or a view on Roth versus traditional.

Before you click file

Preview the return. Confirm that W-2 wages are present, the taxable fellowship appears on Schedule 1 line 8r when applicable, estimated payments are recorded, and no unexpected Schedule C, Schedule SE, or Form 5329 appears. Then check the Indiana return: the fellowship usually carries through from federal adjusted gross income, but deductions and county tax are state-specific.

06 · Where to verify and ask for help

Use primary sources; screen paid help

Purdue fellowship office

Ask how your specific award is classified, paid, and reported. This is the right place for award mechanics, not personalized return preparation.

Purdue Tax Services

For questions about Purdue’s reporting and tax documents, contact tax@purdue.edu.

IRS and Indiana

Use the current forms, instructions, payment pages, and publications. Do not use an old blog post or this guide to calculate and file.

Qualified tax preparer

A CPA or enrolled agent can be helpful for a transition-year review, especially with other income, a spouse, dependents, complex credits, or uncertainty about award treatment.

Questions to ask a preparer

  1. Have you prepared returns for graduate students with taxable fellowship income that was not reported on a W-2?
  2. How would you report taxable scholarship or fellowship income on Form 1040? You should hear a discussion of Schedule 1, line 8r when applicable; it should not be an automatic move to Schedule C.
  3. Can you project federal and Indiana estimated payments for a year that includes assistantship wages and then a fellowship?
  4. Can you review Indiana renter’s deduction and county-tax implications for my address and January 1 residence?
